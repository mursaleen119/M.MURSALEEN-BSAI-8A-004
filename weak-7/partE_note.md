# Task E — Parameters, counterfactual, and ethics

**Message:** Network punctuality improved by 1.73 percentage points from 2025 Q2 to 2026 Q2, but all four modes worsened; the apparent improvement is a service-mix reversal, not better operation within modes. The dashboard is ordered for the Secretary first (aggregate, immediately tested by the adjacent mode panel), then the board's corridor question and the finance director's fare question.

Punctuality is calculated as the sum of valid on-time departures divided by the sum of operated departures, not as an average of daily percentages. Invalid returns where on-time departures exceed operated departures are excluded from both numerator and denominator. The map uses the 12 months ending 2026-08-31, boardings per 1,000 district residents, and five quantile classes. Boundaries are projected using Lambert azimuthal equal-area centered at 70°E, 30°N. The fare comparison uses the 17 complete months from 2025-04 through 2026-08; incomplete 2026-09 is excluded. It indexes journeys and PKR revenue separately to April 2025=100; neither series is combined with boardings.

**Counterfactual:** A raw-count map would have pointed first to D17, D09, and D13; the normalized rate map's top three are D12, D21, and D09. Only D09 appears on both lists. Boardings and population correlate at **r=+0.945**, whereas boardings per 1,000 residents and population correlate at **r=+0.249**. The five most populous districts contain 59.9% of residents on 12.6% of the land. Raw counts therefore largely select population centers; the rate view identifies intensity relative to the resident denominator. These are screening signals, not a stand-alone corridor-siting decision.

**Ethics:** The source data are synthetic and describe a fictional federation. They must not be cited as real-world transit facts. Missing returns are unknown, not zero; route boardings repeated across districts overlap and cannot be summed as unique network riders. A high district rate is not proof of unmet need, and this screen must be combined with accessibility, capacity, equity, cost, and local consultation before infrastructure decisions.
