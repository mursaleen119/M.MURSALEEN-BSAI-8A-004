# Task E — Parameters and limits

The screen's message is that average life expectancy rose from 49.1 to 67.0 years between 1952 and 2007, while all five continent means also rose. It is an adaptation for readers of the existing Gapminder portfolio, not a transit authority decision aid. The trend uses all 12 five-year observations and the arithmetic mean across country rows; the dashboard also shows a population-weighted world mean so the denominator is explicit. The comparison and distribution panels use 2007 country observations. GDP per capita uses a logarithmic x-axis because its positive range is highly skewed. Continent means are unweighted country means; the composition panel uses each continent's share of total resident population in 1952 and 2007.

The original task's reversal is not present for this indicator and window: the equal-country mean rose by 17.95 years, the population-weighted mean by 19.97 years, and each continent mean rose by 11.46–24.41 years. The dashboard shows that check instead of manufacturing an opposite trend. The counterfactual district map is also unavailable: Gapminder has neither the instructor's transit boardings nor boundaries. A raw-population ranking is not a substitute for a transit-demand map, and this work makes no corridor-siting claim. No district suppression or reader-study results are invented.

**Ethics:** country aggregates conceal within-country inequality and do not establish that income causes longevity. Avoid using this screen to rank individual people or make health-resource decisions without local, current, and appropriately disaggregated evidence.
