# Task C — Reliability budget figure and counterfactual

**Reader and message.** For the National Grid Authority budget board: interruption frequency per billed household varies materially by district. The three highest normalized rates are D12, D11, D13; this is a screening result, not a complete outage-severity or service-equity measure.

**Measure and denominator.** Annual recorded supply interruptions divided by billed domestic households, multiplied by 1,000. The measure is interruptions per 1,000 households, not interruption minutes or population. The pooled national rate is 43.7 interruptions per 1,000 households.

**Parameters.** The map uses five quantile classes with breaks 30.43, 40.02, 41.46, 43.17, 48.12, 50.87 (inclusive lower endpoints). District polygons are projected with Lambert azimuthal equal-area centered at 70°E, 30°N before drawing; this preserves area comparisons. The sorted bar panel uses the same normalized measure and a zero baseline. No district was suppressed or merged; all 24 are shown.

**Counterfactual and ethics.** Mapping raw interruption counts would point first to D17, D13, D24, while the rate map points to D12, D11, D13; only D13 appears on both lists. A count-first allocation would direct attention toward the largest district totals and away from high-rate districts D12 and D11. Counts correlate with household totals at r=+0.991, while the normalized rate correlation is r=+0.175. The five most populous districts contain 59.9% of residents on 12.6% of the land, so an area-colored map also gives these dense districts little visual area. No district is suppressed; the source data are synthetic, and this rate screen must be combined with duration, outage concentration, equity and local context before money is allocated.
